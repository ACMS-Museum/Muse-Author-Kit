# Muse Content Style Guide

Muse content should feel like an explorable museum world: concrete, curious,
and responsive to visitor action.

## Tone

- Write for visitors who may not know the machinery or history yet.
- Prefer specific sensory detail over long exposition.
- Let objects and actions reveal story gradually.
- Keep room descriptions short enough to scan during play.

## Rooms and objects

- Give each room a distinct purpose.
- Put important interactive objects in the room description.
- Use aliases for natural visitor wording.
- Make failed actions useful where possible: a blocked action can still hint at
  what the visitor has learned.

## Verbs and puzzles

- Use verbs that a visitor would naturally try.
- Keep puzzle state visible through descriptions, examination text, or hints.
- Avoid requiring exact wording unless the source clearly teaches it.
- Test both the successful path and likely wrong attempts.

## Canon and administration

- Treat shared setting details as maintained canon.
- Do not introduce major setting facts, named institutions, or permanent world
  events without review.
- Keep jokes, references, and anachronisms compatible with the agreed exhibit
  tone.
- Prefer adding new local detail to overwriting existing shared assumptions.
